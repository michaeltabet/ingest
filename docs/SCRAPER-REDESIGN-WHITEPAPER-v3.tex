\begin{abstract}
\noindent A ground-up redesign of the job-board scraping platform (currently atlas-kt: Kotlin + Temporal,
$\sim$13{,}269 employer boards across 13+ ATS platforms, growing). The new system is Python, built from five
independent parts: a scraper library, ClickHouse storage, Temporal execution, an Airflow
janitor/calibrator, and a Django control plane. Doctrine: \emph{extract don't parse; fail loud, handle
daily; no invented constants --- every sizing is a hypothesis audited by data; observability means
validation gates, not dashboards of counts.} The two systems run in parallel under a strict board-ownership
rule until cutover, platform by platform.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Problem}

The current system works and is a black box to its owner. Measured facts, not impressions:

\begin{itemize}
\item 56 scraper classes implement a raw interface directly. \textbf{Zero shared base class.} The original
Python system had a \texttt{BaseScraper}; the Kotlin port deliberately flattened it into every file.
\item \textbf{37 scrapers carry a copy-pasted \texttt{stripHtml()}; 42 carry their own \texttt{sha256Hex()}.}
Every scraper re-implements pagination, retries, and date handling. One behavioral fix touches up to 42 files.
\item The owner cannot read Kotlin/JVM code. All inspection and repair routes through an intermediary ---
unacceptable for a system whose steady-state workload \emph{is} repair (\S\ref{sec:drift}).
\item \textbf{Silent failure history.} 2026-07-02: $\sim$500 boards reported success while publishing nothing
(empty bodies validated cleanly; \texttt{jobs=[]} passes a schema check).
\item \textbf{Retry lockup history.} 2026-07-03: unlimited retries on one permanently-403'd board froze an
entire nightly run after $\sim$36 boards.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Decision:} build a new system in Python; run both in parallel under strict board ownership
(\S\ref{sec:ownership}). This is not a port of code --- it is a port of \emph{knowledge}: each platform's
quirks are documented facts in the old code's comments, paid for by incidents, and they transfer as facts.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Doctrine}
\label{sec:doctrine}

Five commitments that settle later arguments:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Readable by the owner.} Python; a hierarchy of at most three levels; every behavior has
exactly one home file (\S\ref{sec:whereis}).
\item \textbf{Extract, don't parse.} Scrapers land raw bytes. Parsing is a separate, replayable batch
concern, deliberately deferred.
\item \textbf{Fail loud, handle daily.} No defensive tuning inside Temporal. If something breaks, it breaks
visibly; a daily Airflow pass reads the evidence and deals with it. We would rather a red workflow and a
notification than a silent constraint.
\item \textbf{No invented constants.} Any number (slots, batch sizes, budgets) is a \emph{hypothesis},
recorded as such, audited and re-sized by data on a schedule (\S\ref{sec:calibration}). Laws are not knobs;
knobs are not laws.
\item \textbf{Observability = validation gates.} Not counters, not vanity dashboards: explicit assertions
with defined failure actions (\S\ref{sec:gates}). If a signal does not change a decision, it does not exist.
\end{enumerate}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The Five Hard Problems}

\subsection{Drift --- pages change under us}
\label{sec:drift}
ATS platforms change APIs, markup, and anti-bot posture continuously. Consequences: a platform change must
touch exactly one file (blast radius); breakage must state \emph{where and what} (``workday: detail fetch
403, 0/1400 jobs'' --- never ``run complete~\checkmark''); repair must be reproducible offline from recorded
fixtures (\S\ref{sec:tests}).

\subsection{Cost --- boards are not equal}
A Greenhouse board is 1 request; a 1{,}400-job Workday board is $\sim$1{,}407. Budgeting must be per-board
and learned from history (\S\ref{sec:scheduling}).

\subsection{Redundant compute --- the dedup tradeoff}
Blind re-scraping re-fetches thousands of unchanged job details nightly. Details are $\sim$99.5\% of a heavy
board's cost; $\sim$95\% of stubs are unchanged day-over-day. Resolved by incremental extraction
(\S\ref{sec:dedup}) without compromising the raw-first architecture.

\subsection{Growth --- boards keep arriving}
The employer list grows continuously. Every run reads the board table fresh at start; a board added in the
Django admin is in that night's run. No deploys; no magic numbers anywhere in the architecture.

\subsection{Orchestration overhead at scale}
Per-board workflows at 13K+ boards $\approx$ 250--350K Temporal history events per night against Temporal's
Postgres --- on a cluster with a documented HDD IOPS starvation failure mode. Task granularity is a sized
hypothesis (\S\ref{sec:temporal}).

\section{Part 1 --- The Scraper Library}

\subsection{The six acquisition families}
The classification that matters is \emph{how the data is acquired} --- the family is where shared code lives:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}clll@{}}
\toprule
\# & Family & Shape & Examples \\
\midrule
1 & ONE-SHOT & one call returns everything & Greenhouse, Lever \\
2 & PAGED & walk pages, each complete & Recruitee-style \\
3 & PAGED+DETAIL & pages of stubs, then per-job detail & Workday, iCIMS \\
4 & HTML & no API; parse served HTML & older ATSes \\
5 & BROWSER & JS-built page; render headless & Paradox (the only one today) \\
6 & BROWSER+SESSION & login / anti-bot & none yet --- slot reserved \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Ground truth from the current registry: \textbf{54 of 55 platforms are plain HTTP}. Browser scraping is a
bolt-on, not the core. \emph{Protection level} (Cloudflare, TLS fingerprint, headers) is deliberately not a
family --- it is client configuration on any family; folding it in would mint a dozen fake families.

\subsection{The class hierarchy --- three levels}
\begin{verbatim}
L1  Scraper                          contract: (board, ctx) -> RawResult
L2  OneShotScraper / PagedScraper / PagedDetailScraper /
    HtmlScraper / RenderScraper / SessionScraper       <- THE LOOPS, once each
L3  WorkdayScraper(PagedDetailScraper) etc.            <- platform FACTS only
\end{verbatim}

Transport (\texttt{HttpClient}, \texttt{BrowserClient}) is \emph{composition}: injected via a context
object, never inherited. Family loops call \texttt{ctx.http}; they never construct clients; tests inject a
fixture-replaying fake.

Rules that keep the tree from rotting:
\begin{itemize}
\item \law{Family = mechanism, platform = facts.} An \texttt{if platform == "workday"} inside a family file
is a design failure; that logic is a platform override.
\item \law{Inheritance only for the template chain.} Utilities (date parse, HTML strip, digest) are pure
functions in \texttt{normalize.py} --- never mixins, never base-class methods.
\item \law{The pagination hook is a cursor:} \texttt{parse\_list(resp) $\rightarrow$ (stubs, cursor|None)}.
Covers offset (Workday), page-number, token, and one-shot (always \texttt{None}) with a single shape. No
platform overrides the loop itself.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Where-is-what: the locality contract}
\label{sec:whereis}
Readability is enforceable only if every question has a one-file answer. This table ships in the repo README:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
``Where is\ldots?'' & Exactly one home \\
\midrule
rate caps / headers / TLS / connection handling & \texttt{core/http.py} \\
the scraping loop for a family & \texttt{core/families/<family>.py} \\
a platform's URLs, cursor, quirks & \texttt{platforms/<platform>.py} \\
date/HTML/digest utilities & \texttt{core/normalize.py} \\
what a scraper returns & \texttt{core/models.py} \\
error taxonomy & \texttt{core/errors.py} \\
platform $\rightarrow$ class mapping & \texttt{platforms/registry.py} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

If a behavior ever has two plausible homes, that is a design bug to fix, not a judgment call to live with.

\subsection{Evidence-carrying results}
\label{sec:evidence}
\texttt{fetch()} returns payloads \emph{plus} the run's evidence:
\begin{verbatim}
RawResult: payloads[], http_status, pages_fetched, stubs_seen,
           details_ok, details_failed, bytes_in, errors[]
\end{verbatim}
Judgment happens \emph{outside} the scraper, against this evidence (\S\ref{sec:gates}). Zero rows is
distinguishable from success at the type level; the 2026-07-02 silent-success failure class becomes
structurally impossible.

\subsection{The test story --- CI gates, not conventions}
\label{sec:tests}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \gate{No platform exists without fixtures.} A registry-coverage test walks \texttt{platforms/} and
fails CI if any platform lacks: $\geq$1 recorded list response, $\geq$1 recorded detail response (family~3),
and an expected \texttt{RawResult} snapshot.
\item \textbf{Recording is a one-liner:} \texttt{scrape record <platform> --board <id>}. Re-recording after
drift is the \emph{first} repair step --- the fixture diff usually is the diagnosis.
\item \textbf{Family contract tests} exercise each loop once against synthetic fixtures (empty page, short
last page, cursor loop, partial detail failures); platforms do not re-test the loop.
\item \textbf{Standalone runnability:} \texttt{scrape run <platform> --board <id> --dry} --- no Temporal, no
ClickHouse; evidence printed to stdout. This is the 2am tool.
\end{enumerate}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Part 2 --- Storage (ClickHouse)}
\label{sec:storage}

\subsection{Tables}
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{scrape\_raw} --- append-only, immutable: \texttt{platform, board\_id, url, kind(list|detail),
http\_status, fetched\_at, run\_id, payload, digest(sha256), stub\_digest}. \texttt{stub\_digest} exists
from day one (\S\ref{sec:dedup} depends on it; painful to backfill).
\item \texttt{scrape\_evidence} --- one row per board per run: the \texttt{RawResult} counts + outcome.
This table is simultaneously the cost ledger, the gate input, and the observability source.
\item \texttt{temporal\_meta} --- CDC-replicated Temporal workflow metadata (\S\ref{sec:cdc}).
\item \texttt{gate\_results} --- every gate evaluation (\S\ref{sec:gates}) is itself a row: gates are data.
\end{itemize}

Engine note: \texttt{ReplacingMergeTree} merges lazily --- any dedup-dependent read (seen-sets) deduplicates
on read (\texttt{GROUP BY}/\texttt{FINAL}), always per-board, never a global scan.

\subsection{Medallion layout}
\texttt{BRONZE = scrape\_raw} (immutable) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{SILVER = jobs\_parsed} (batch, later,
replayable from Bronze at zero re-scrape cost) $\rightarrow$ \texttt{GOLD} (product-facing).
\textbf{Boundary law:} scrape phase = everything that needs the \emph{network}; batch phase = everything
that needs only the \emph{bytes}. Detail-fetching needs the network $\rightarrow$ scraper. Field mapping
needs only bytes $\rightarrow$ batch.

\subsection{Temporal metadata via CDC}
\label{sec:cdc}
Requirement: all Temporal execution metadata queryable in ClickHouse next to the scrape evidence.
Pipeline: \textbf{Debezium (Kafka Connect) on Temporal's Postgres $\rightarrow$ Kafka $\rightarrow$
ClickHouse}.

\textbf{The one crucial aiming instruction:} Temporal's core history tables store serialized protobuf blobs
--- CDC on those yields unreadable garbage. The \emph{visibility} tables
(\texttt{executions\_visibility}) are plain rows: workflow id, type, status, start/close times, failure
summary. \textbf{CDC the visibility tables.} Result: every workflow lifecycle event lands in
\texttt{temporal\_meta}, joinable to \texttt{scrape\_evidence} by run and board.

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Part 3 --- Temporal, Fail-Loud}
\label{sec:temporal}

Temporal's whole job: \textbf{schedule, fan out, run, record.} It is built to fail visibly; when it breaks,
we step in, figure it out, and go. No defensive tuning.

\subsection{Topology}
\begin{verbatim}
Schedule (per platform, staggered)
  -> PlatformRun parent workflow      (one per platform, never a mega-parent)
       activity plan_run  -> frozen RunPlan (board list + seen-sets + sizes)
       -> children: cheap families batched N boards/child (N is a sized
          hypothesis); heavy families (paged+detail, browser) 1 board/child
\end{verbatim}
Parent-per-platform keeps each history far below Temporal's $\sim$50K-event cap and matches the real failure
domain (platforms break platform-wide). The parent does not wait on stragglers; a run finishes with its
failures recorded, not resolved.

\subsection{The six things to know --- reclassified}
Two are laws, one is off by doctrine, three are ratios owned by the calibrator (\S\ref{sec:calibration}):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}llX@{}}
\toprule
\# & Item & Status \\
\midrule
1 & Workflow vs Activity & \law{LAW.} Workflows decide (deterministic); activities do (network, disk).
No HTTP, no clocks, no randomness in workflow code. \\
2 & Worker slots & \textbf{HYPOTHESIS --- never a guard.} Start from a stated guess. An OOM-kill is a
\emph{loud, recorded event}, not a disaster to prevent upfront: k8s restarts the pod, the interrupted
boards surface as ghosts (\gate{G2}), and the calibrator resizes from the data
(\S\ref{sec:calibration}). Nothing is defensively capped. \\
3 & Heartbeat & \textbf{OFF by doctrine.} No dead-worker detector in Temporal. The daily absence gate
(\gate{G2}) detects ghosts at daily latency --- accepted deliberately. \\
4 & \texttt{start\_to\_close} & \textbf{Absurd by design} (30 days --- the SDK requires \emph{some} value;
ours exists but never fires). Duration drift is reported by the calibrator, not enforced by a leash. \\
5 & Retries & \textbf{MANUAL.} \texttt{maximum\_attempts=1} explicitly --- Temporal's \emph{default is
infinite retries}, so ``no policy'' must be written down as one attempt. Failure lands as data; Airflow
re-runs on its cadence. \\
6 & Payloads & \law{LAW.} Activities write raw data to ClickHouse themselves and return only counts. Data
never travels through Temporal --- Temporal carries decisions and numbers. If the law is broken, Temporal
breaks loudly (2MB hard cap) and the audit gate (\gate{G7}) names the culprit. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\subsection{Determinism and the RunPlan}
Workflows never read live config (determinism). The first activity \texttt{plan\_run} snapshots policy +
ledger into a frozen \textbf{RunPlan}, which the parent executes and which is written to ClickHouse.
Every run records exactly the plan it ran under: a weird Thursday is
\texttt{diff(RunPlan\_thu, RunPlan\_wed)}, not archaeology.

\section{Part 4 --- Airflow: Janitor, Calibrator, Auditor}
\label{sec:airflow}

Airflow (Apache Foundation --- consistent with the standing foundation-bias tool rule) runs on
KubernetesExecutor: each task is a pod that lives and dies; idle Airflow is just scheduler + webserver +
its metadata Postgres. It has three roles and owns all judgment in the system.

\subsection{Janitor (daily)}
Reads \texttt{scrape\_evidence} + \texttt{temporal\_meta}: which boards failed, which are absent, which
gates fired. Re-runs failures by starting fresh Temporal workflows (never by resurrecting old ones). Runs
the Bronze $\rightarrow$ Silver parse via \texttt{KubernetesPodOperator}.

\subsection{Calibrator (weekly, or on demand)}
\label{sec:calibration}
Closes the loop on \emph{no invented constants}. Every sized quantity is a hypothesis row (value,
rationale, date); calibration DAGs recompute recommendations from data:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXX@{}}
\toprule
Quantity & Signal & Computation \\
\midrule
Worker slots per queue & p95 memory per scrape per family; observed OOM-kill events & recommend the next
hypothesis from data (OOMs included as signal, not treated as failure to prevent) \\
Batch size per platform & EWMA board duration & boards per child s.t.\ child duration
$\approx$ target \\
Nightly budget spend & requests + seconds per platform per night & report spend vs.\ intent; flag
platforms starving the floor rule \\
Re-run cadence value & success-rate-on-rerun & 90\% pass on rerun $\Rightarrow$ blips, daily is right;
20\% $\Rightarrow$ those boards are dying differently \\
Duration drift & p50/p99 per family per platform & report only --- no leash to tighten \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Recommendations become updated policy rows (read by the next \texttt{plan\_run}) or, when a change implies
k8s resources, a \emph{proposed gitops MR} --- Airflow never mutates the cluster directly.
Every derived value carries hardcoded \emph{clamps} (min/max bounds): bounds are physics, values are
learned; one weird night must not teach the system garbage.

\subsection{Scheduling brain (computed nightly, executed by Temporal)}
\label{sec:scheduling}
The classic crawler-revisit problem, solved the boring way:
\begin{itemize}
\item Per board, EWMAs from the ledger: \texttt{cost\_b} (requests + seconds), \texttt{yield\_b}
(\emph{new} jobs), \texttt{score\_b = yield\_b / cost\_b}.
\item \textbf{Greedy knapsack per platform:} sort boards by score, scrape until the platform's nightly
budget (a human \emph{intent}, set in Django) is spent.
\item \gate{Floor rule (non-negotiable):} every board scraped at least once every $N$ days regardless of
score --- a dead board can wake up, and without the floor the model never learns it did. $N$ is itself
calibrated from observed wake-up rates per platform.
\end{itemize}
Emergent, zero per-board config: hot boards drift to nightly, dead boards decay to weekly, newly-churning
boards are promoted within $\sim$3 runs. Chronic failers deprioritize \emph{themselves}: their cost rises,
their yield is zero, their score collapses --- economics is the retry policy across nights; the floor
guarantees nobody is forgotten forever.

\subsection{Incremental extraction (the dedup mechanism)}
\label{sec:dedup}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Always scrape lists blind} --- cheap; keeps the index complete.
\item \textbf{Fetch details only for unseen stubs.} \texttt{plan\_run} queries the board's known
\texttt{stub\_digest} set (per-board, dedup-on-read) and passes it \emph{into} the activity. The scraper
stays a pure function \texttt{(board, known\_digests, ctx) $\rightarrow$ RawResult}; ClickHouse owns state.
\item \textbf{Periodic full re-fetch} per board heals the drift incremental extraction cannot see (a job
edited without its stub changing). The interval is calibrated from measured edit rates: on every full
re-fetch, count how many ``unchanged'' stubs had changed details --- the refetch generates its own tuning
signal.
\end{enumerate}
Effect: $\sim$95\% compute cut on steady boards, compounding with \S\ref{sec:scheduling}. Accepted loss:
silently-edited details are stale up to the refetch interval; job ads are post/expire dominated.

\subsection{Cold start --- eliminated structurally}
Boards arrive only via ownership flips, platform-by-platform (\S\ref{sec:ownership}); the planner is seeded
from atlas-kt history and known job counts; politeness caps live in the HTTP client and bind regardless of
planner state. The system is never simultaneously big and ignorant.

\section{Part 5 --- Django Control Plane}
Boards inventory (the growing table), per-platform switches (kill switch: pause a platform NOW from the
admin), \textbf{owner} per board (\S\ref{sec:ownership}), and the human \emph{intents}: nightly budget per
platform, schedule times. Everything else that was once ``config'' is either a law (in code), a doctrine
(written here), or a calibrated hypothesis (computed by Airflow). The admin edits intents; the data audits
them.

\section{Observability = Validation Gates}
\label{sec:gates}

Doctrine: \emph{a signal that does not change a decision does not exist.} No per-request metrics, no
latency histograms of scraper internals, no log aggregation beyond what Temporal keeps, no new metrics
stack: \textbf{the ledger is the telemetry}; Grafana reads ClickHouse; the Temporal UI shows live state.

Observability here is a chain of \textbf{gates} --- explicit assertions with defined actions. Every gate
evaluation writes a row to \texttt{gate\_results}: the gates themselves are data, so ``did the gates run''
is itself checkable.

\subsection{The gate chain}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lXXl@{}}
\toprule
Gate & Assertion & On failure & Where \\
\midrule
\gate{G1 Delivery} & child run: HTTP 200-class \emph{and} \texttt{stubs\_seen} $>$ 0 on any board that had
$>$ 0 last run & workflow marked \textbf{FAILED}, visibly red --- never a warning & Temporal child \\
\addlinespace
\gate{G2 Presence} & every board in last night's RunPlan has an evidence row today & board flagged
\emph{ghost}; re-run queued; this \emph{is} the dead-worker detector (daily latency, by doctrine) &
Airflow daily \\
\addlinespace
\gate{G3 Volume} & per platform: tonight's new-jobs count within historical band ($3\sigma$ of trailing 30
runs) --- \emph{and} not $\approx$0 across all boards & \textbf{silent-success alarm} (the 2026-07-02
class, now watched, not just discouraged) & Airflow daily \\
\addlinespace
\gate{G4 Landing} & \texttt{scrape\_raw} row count reconciles with \texttt{details\_ok} claimed in
evidence; digests unique & discrepancy alarm --- evidence is lying or writes were lost & Airflow daily \\
\addlinespace
\gate{G5 Parse} & Bronze $\rightarrow$ Silver: parse-error rate within platform baseline; row counts
reconcile; unknown-field ratio below drift threshold & parse halted for that platform; Bronze untouched;
fix parser, replay & Airflow, per parse \\
\addlinespace
\gate{G6 Ownership} & no board has scrapes from both systems within 24h outside a declared parity window &
loud page --- this protects the \emph{old} system's stability & Airflow daily \\
\addlinespace
\gate{G7 Temporal audit} & (via CDC) no workflow red without a matching evidence row; no history/payload
size growing abnormally (= data leaking through Temporal, law \#6 broken) & named-culprit alarm & Airflow
weekly \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\subsection{Surfaces --- capped}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One dashboard} (one screen, no scrolling): one row per platform --- boards planned / scraped
/ failed, new jobs, budget spent vs intent, gate summary. Green row = look away. Ten seconds over coffee.
\item \textbf{One drill-down query} (parameterized, saved): failed/ghost boards for a platform with error
class and evidence counts. Pull, never push.
\item \textbf{One weekly trend view:} yield, cost per board, permanent-error rate, duration drift per
platform over 30 days. Slow rot (Cloudflare tightening, an API decaying) shows here before it is an incident.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Alerts --- four, total}
Humans are interrupted only by: \textbf{(1)} run absent (platform's nightly run didn't start/finish);
\textbf{(2)} G3 volume/silent-success; \textbf{(3)} G6 ownership violation; \textbf{(4)} G2 ghost-rate
above platform baseline. Per-board failures are weather --- the janitor handles them; nobody is paged.
\textbf{Adding a fifth alert requires deleting one.} That rule is the whole discipline.

\section{Deployment on Kubernetes}
\label{sec:deploy}

All changes via gitops MRs reconciled by ArgoCD --- nothing is ever applied by hand. Internal GUIs
(Airflow, Django admin) are tailnet-only behind the standard SSO path. Images are built on the Mac
(pinned tags, tailnet registry --- CI has no docker runner), \texttt{imagePullPolicy: IfNotPresent}.
The architecture map is updated in the same unit of work as every deploy --- a change without a map update
is incomplete.

\subsection{Reused (zero new stateful infrastructure)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Temporal cluster} --- already live; new namespace + two task queues
(\texttt{scrape-http}, \texttt{scrape-browser}).
\item \textbf{ClickHouse} --- the existing instance; new \texttt{scrape} database (\S\ref{sec:storage}).
\item \textbf{Kafka} --- already present; carries the CDC stream.
\item \textbf{Keycloak + tailnet ingress} --- standard auth for the two GUIs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{New components}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Scrape workers} --- one Python image (scraper library + Temporal worker); two Deployments
(http / browser). Replica counts, slots, and memory limits start as stated hypotheses and are re-sized
from observed data --- including OOM events --- by the calibrator (\S\ref{sec:calibration}); k8s restarts
are part of the design, not incidents.
\item \textbf{Airflow} --- official Apache chart, KubernetesExecutor, CNPG metadata Postgres.
\item \textbf{Debezium Connect} --- on the existing Kafka, aimed at Temporal's \emph{visibility} tables.
\item \textbf{Django} --- one Deployment + CNPG Postgres.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Rollout order (one MR per step; map updated each step)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item ClickHouse tables (minutes, zero risk).
\item Repo + CI (fixture gates) + worker image --- scrapes nothing yet.
\item Worker Deployment + ONE platform schedule (greenhouse: simplest family).
\item Parity window vs.\ atlas-kt on a $\leq$25-board sample (\S\ref{sec:ownership}).
\item Airflow + the daily janitor DAG + gates G2--G6.
\item Debezium CDC + gate G7 + the calibrator DAGs.
\item Flip greenhouse ownership; proceed platform-by-platform in board-count order.
\end{enumerate}

%------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Parallel Running --- the Ownership Rule}
\label{sec:ownership}

Two systems hitting the same boards from the same egress IPs can trip rate limits and make the \emph{old,
working} system appear to degrade. Therefore, a rule, not advice:

\begin{quote}
\law{THE OWNERSHIP RULE.} Every board row carries \texttt{owner $\in$ \{atlas-kt, new\}}. A board is
scraped by its owner and \emph{only} its owner. No board is ever double-scraped --- except inside a
declared parity window.
\end{quote}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Parity window} (per platform, during cutover only): $\leq$25 sample boards, both systems,
runs $\geq$6h apart, new-system politeness cap halved, time-boxed ($\sim$7 nights), then closed.
\item \textbf{Cutover per platform:} port $\rightarrow$ fixture + parity gates green $\rightarrow$ flip
\texttt{owner} for that platform's boards in one transaction. Rollback = flip back.
\item \textbf{Enforcement is gate G6}, not good intentions.
\item The old system needs zero code changes: its board list is already DB-driven; ownership filters at
plan time.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Port discipline (mechanical, not aspirational):} port order = board count descending (top $\sim$10
platforms cover $\sim$80\% of boards); ``ported'' is a \emph{defined state} (fixtures present + standalone
run green + parity green $N$ nights); one platform in flight at a time; \textbf{scheduler work beyond
greedy+floor is forbidden until the top-10 platforms are live} --- any earlier scheduler sophistication is
procrastination wearing an algorithm costume.

\textbf{Residual risks (execution, not architecture):} (1) the port grind dominates the calendar --- the
gates manage it, they don't shrink it; (2) discipline rules (no \texttt{if platform ==} in family files,
four alerts max, the scheduler gate) are enforced by CI where possible and by the owner's discipline
everywhere else --- this is the single most likely path back to atlas-kt-shaped rot; (3) seeded priors can
be wrong for platforms the old system handled badly --- the floor rule bounds the damage to $N$ days of
staleness, and the first scrape corrects the model.

\section{Summary}

\begin{quote}
Idempotent, incrementally-extracting ingestion jobs land immutable raw payloads to ClickHouse with
content-hash keys and ingestion-time evidence; a seeded, self-calibrating planner schedules boards by
yield-per-cost under human budget intents with a max-staleness floor; Temporal executes frozen per-run
plans loudly and dumbly through family-templated, platform-thin scraper classes behind a hard
board-ownership boundary; Airflow judges everything daily through explicit validation gates; and parsing
is a replayable downstream batch concern.
\end{quote}

Every sentence above was measured in the current codebase, paid for by a recorded incident, or is a
standard pattern chosen over a clever one on purpose --- and the v3 revisions (fail-loud Temporal, the
calibrator, the gate chain) came from adversarial review of v1 and v2, on purpose too.
